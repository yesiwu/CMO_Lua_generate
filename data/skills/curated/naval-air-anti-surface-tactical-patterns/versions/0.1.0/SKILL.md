# Anti-Surface Strike Tactical Patterns

## Quick Reference
- **Preserve strikers**: Don't expose aircraft before they can deliver weapons; time ingress to arrive at weapon release with full combat capability.
- **Route for survivability**: Choose paths that minimize radar/optical exposure while retaining ingress/egress options; avoid predictable straight lines.
- **Hold ordnance reserve**: Don't expend all weapons on first pass; retain enough for re-attack or defensive needs.
- **Deconflict targets**: Assign one primary target per unit/aircraft group; verify no naval surface fires and air strike overlap.

## When To Use
- When launching coordinated air strikes against surface groups with organic air defense or SAM coverage.
- When strike packages must penetrate contested battlespace with limited tanker or escort availability.
- When multiple friendly surface units are within weapon range of the same target set.
- When mission requires sustained pressure over multiple waves rather than a single max-effort salvo.

## Strategy Patterns

### Strike Asset Preservation
#### Maintain Formation Integrity
- Keep strikers in mutually supporting elements; avoid single-ship or single-pair exceptions unless terrain/weather masks exist.
- Delay separation until outside expected threat rings; reform after weapon release for mutual defense.
- Use standoff weapons if available; reduce time inside lethal envelope.

#### Time-On-Target Synchronization
- Coordinate ingress timing so all strikers arrive at weapon-release near-simultaneously; saturate but do not linger.
- Set earliest/latest release windows; abort early if timing slips beyond tolerance to avoid sequential attrition.

### Route Exposure Control
#### Avoid Predictable Geometry
- Use terrain masking or radar shadowing on ingress; offset approach azimuth from target bearing.
- Insert waypoint turns near known sensor boundaries; fly parallel to threat axes rather than directly at them.
- Egress on different heading than ingress; avoid retracing exposed path.

#### Kinematic Deconfliction from Defenders
- Route around known CAP stations or surface pickets; if unavoidable, schedule transit during gaps or with escort support.
- Maintain altitude/speed profile that minimizes detectability until final run; descend only inside weapon employment zone.

### Ordnance Reserve Management
#### Allocate for Follow-On
- Fire only enough weapons to achieve mission objective per target; retain at least one load per aircraft for re-attack or self-defense.
- Prioritize high-value targets first; do not expend all ordnance on secondary units.

#### Cue for Re-Attack
- Preserve target coordinates and damage assessment capability; if first strike incomplete, re-attack with remaining assets.
- Do not jettison or expend ordnance without contact; hold until positive ID or terminal cue.

### Target Deconfliction
#### Single-Owner Assignment
- Assign each primary target to exactly one shooter (aircraft group or surface unit); record in plan.
- Cross-check before weapon release: no two units should have same target in their fire control systems.

#### Conflict Resolution Rules
- If duplication discovered, higher-value asset (e.g., aircraft with longer-range weapons) keeps target; lower-value breaks off.
- When in doubt, use geographic separation—assign targets by sectors or corridors to avoid overlap.

## Counterexamples
- **Do not** route directly from launch to target over open water if a longer waypoint path reduces exposure; straight-line time savings rarely offset attrition risk.
- **Avoid** launching all aircraft with full ordnance in one wave if follow-on waves are possible; retaining reserve is not wasteful.
- **Never** let two units engage the same target unless explicitly coordinated for split attack (e.g., simultaneous front/back bearing); unplanned duplicate fire wastes assets.
- **Don't** delay strike to achieve perfect timing if it keeps aircraft loitering in threat zone; early but imperfect is better than late and lost.
